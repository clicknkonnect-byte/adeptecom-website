How to choose a TikTok Shop agency in 2026 (buyer’s guide)

Strategy7 September 2026·7 min read

TikTok Shop looks like a simple channel from the outside — list a product, get creators posting, watch GMV climb. In practice it’s five different jobs running at once: compliance, catalogue, creator sourcing, paid amplification, and LIVE, each with its own failure modes. Most brands don’t find out which agency actually does all five until months in, after a rejected shop, a stalled affiliate program, or a reporting dashboard that never quite matches revenue. Here’s what to check before you sign, so you find out before the contract instead of after.

Ask what happens after setup, not just during it

Almost every agency can get a shop live. Far fewer stay on the account once it’s approved. The real test isn’t the onboarding call — it’s what the relationship looks like in month three, when the initial push has faded and the shop needs continuous listing work, creator refresh, and ad iteration to keep compounding instead of plateauing.

  • Ask directly: “What does your team do on my account in a normal week, six months from now?” A vague answer is the answer.
  • Check the contract structure — a one-time setup fee with no ongoing management scope is a signal the relationship ends at go-live.
  • Ask for a current client who has been with them longer than four months, not just a launch case study.

Find out if the creator network is real or rented per campaign

Affiliate GMV is usually the biggest lever on TikTok Shop, and it lives or dies on creator relationships. An agency that sources creators fresh for every client, from scratch, every time, is starting from zero on your account. One with an existing, vetted network can activate relevant creators in days instead of weeks — and can tell which ones actually convert before they ever ship a sample.

  • Ask how big and how vetted the network is, and whether it’s specific to your market (US, UK, or both) and category.
  • Ask how they decide which creator videos get turned into paid Spark Ads — a real process looks at performance data, not gut feel.
  • Ask what offer structure they typically recommend — flat fee, commission, or hybrid — and why. A confident, specific answer beats a generic one.
The agency that already has creators who convert in your category will always outpace the one that has to build that list from scratch on your budget.

Check their compliance track record before, not after, a rejection

TikTok Shop rejects listings for missing SDS documents, wrong category leafs, and unsupported claims far more often than brands expect, and a rejected or suspended shop stalls every other part of the strategy while it’s unresolved. An agency that can walk you through common rejection causes for your specific category, unprompted, has actually done this before. One that treats compliance as an afterthought will treat your rejection the same way — as a support ticket, not a priority.

  • Ask what documentation your category typically requires (SDS, certifications, claim substantiation) before you even sign.
  • Ask how they’ve handled a suspended or rejected shop for a past client, and how long resolution took.

Look past the platform-reported ROAS number

Spark Ads and GMV Max both report return inside TikTok’s own dashboard, and that number is structurally optimistic — it credits the platform generously and doesn’t reconcile against what actually lands in your bank account. An agency that only ever quotes in-platform ROAS either doesn’t track further, or doesn’t want to. Either way, you’ll be making budget decisions on a number that overstates performance.

  • Ask what they measure against besides the in-platform dashboard — blended revenue, true CAC, and GMV per creator are the metrics that actually match your P&L.
  • Ask for a sample report, not a description of one. What gets shown tells you what gets managed.

Weigh price against what it actually buys

The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest outcome once you factor in a stalled launch, a rejected shop sitting idle for weeks, or an affiliate program that never gets past a handful of creators. Compare quotes on scope, not just the monthly number: does the fee include compliance handling, ongoing creator sourcing, ad management, and reporting, or is each of those an add-on you’ll be quoted separately once you’re already committed?

None of this requires taking an agency’s word for it. Ask the questions above in the first call, and you’ll know within twenty minutes whether you’re looking at a team that runs TikTok Shop as a full system, or one that’s going to hand you a live shop and leave the rest to you.
