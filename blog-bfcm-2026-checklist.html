Black Friday & Cyber Monday 2026: the readiness checklist for TikTok Shop and Amazon sellers

Strategy2 November 2026·6 min read

BFCM is the one week of the year where a gap you ignored in October becomes a stockout, a frozen ad set, or a disapproved deal on the day it matters most. Most of the fixes below need days, not hours, to take effect — which is why the window to act on this list is now, not the week of the sale.

Why the next three weeks decide the whole quarter

TikTok Shop and Amazon both front-load the approvals that make a strong BFCM possible: deal submissions, inventory cutoffs, and ad-learning phases all have lead times measured in days or weeks, not hours. A brand that waits until Thanksgiving week to fix any of this is choosing to sell through whatever setup it already has, good or bad. Everything here is ordered so the slowest-moving items — the ones with hard deadlines set by the platforms, not by you — come first.

TikTok Shop: what to lock down now

  • Inventory buffer per SKU — set it for the uplift you actually expect from your promoted SKUs, not your average week. A listing that goes out of stock mid-sale loses its ranking boost right when it matters most.
  • Shop promotions and flash deals submitted early — TikTok Shop deal types need review time before they go live. Submit now so a rejection still leaves time to resubmit.
  • Creator briefs sent, not just planned — your affiliate network needs the offer, the Spark Ads codes, and the posting window with enough notice to actually schedule content, not a last-minute push that gets ignored.
  • GMV Max and Spark Ads budgets scaled gradually beforehand — jumping a campaign's budget 5× on the day resets its learning phase at the worst possible moment. Step it up over the preceding week or two instead.
  • LIVE selling slots booked for the peak days — live traffic and conversion both spike during BFCM; a host and script locked in advance beats a scramble on the day.

We cover the mechanics of the ad side in more depth in our GMV Max guide — the same cold-start problem applies tenfold when budget jumps right before a peak week.

Amazon: what to lock down now

  • FBA inbound shipments hit the fulfillment center before Amazon's published cutoff — this is the single most common BFCM failure: stock that's correct on paper but still in transit when the sale starts.
  • Lightning Deals and other deal types submitted on Amazon's own schedule — these programs run on advance-application windows set by Amazon, not by you; missing the window means missing the placement entirely this cycle.
  • PPC budgets and bid headroom raised ahead of the surge — a daily budget cap that was fine in October will throttle a winning campaign by noon on Black Friday. Check caps, not just bids.
  • Listing and A+ content checked against the live promotion — strike-through pricing and claims need to match what's actually being offered, or the listing risks a compliance flag during the one week you can't afford a suppression.
  • Brand Store ready for the traffic spike — sponsored and organic traffic both jump; a storefront that cross-sells the full range converts more of it than a single product page can.

These are the same structural issues behind most ACOS spikes, covered in detail in our Amazon PPC mistakes post — they just hit harder under BFCM-level traffic.

The guardrails that apply to both channels

  • Pixel, CAPI, and conversion tracking health-checked now — a tracking gap you'd normally catch within a week is a blind spot during the exact days you need the data to make live decisions.
  • Customer service capacity planned for inbox volume — question and complaint volume rises with order volume; staffing or an auto-responder for common questions keeps response times from sliding during the sale.
  • Return and exchange policy visible before checkout — BFCM shoppers convert faster when the policy is clear up front, and it's cheaper to state it than to field disputes afterward.
  • A retention plan for the day after — BFCM brings in a wave of one-time, discount-driven buyers. The list-building, retargeting, and follow-up offer you set up now is what turns that wave into repeat customers in December.

One week out: the final pass

Seven days before the sale, re-check only the things that change fast: live inventory counts against the buffer you set, ad budgets and caps against current spend pace, and that every submitted deal actually shows as approved rather than pending. Everything else on this list should already be done by then — the final week is for catching drift, not for starting the work.
